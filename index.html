Why Reply Rate Is the Metric That Matters

Not all outreach metrics are equal. Here's why we built the benchmark around reply rate.

Connection acceptance rate is easy to inflate — widen your audience, lower the ICP filter, and the number climbs. That makes it a weak quality signal. Reply rate is harder to game: it measures whether a real person, after accepting your connection request, thought your opening message was worth responding to.

In this test, replies were tracked over a 14-day window from each accepted connection. Any intentional response counted — a meeting request, a polite decline, or even a "who are you and how did you find me." The aim was to capture engagement quality, not conversions. Sales cycles at the B2B level run weeks or months, making a 6-week window unsuitable for downstream conversion tracking; reply rate is the right leading indicator to measure.

Accepted connection rate still matters as a secondary metric because it determines the pool size that can reply at all. A 62% acceptance rate with 18.4% replies tells a different story from 52% acceptance with 12.7% replies — but reply rate is the dimension where message quality and sender credibility actually show up in the data.

Account ban risk rounds out the picture. A tool that delivers a 7% reply rate and a medium ban probability is, in practice, a worse deal than those numbers suggest. One restriction mid-campaign resets your account's reputation and delays pipeline for weeks.

How We Ran This Test

Setup, audience segmentation, and reply counting protocol.

The test ran from January 6 to February 14, 2026. Each platform received 400 unique connection requests per week, distributed across four B2B ICP segments: founders of venture-backed B2B SaaS companies, independent agency owners, sales directors at companies with 50 to 500 employees, and operators of venture-backed startups at the Series A or B stage.

Audience data was pulled from LinkedIn Sales Navigator under a single account, then split randomly into four non-overlapping groups of 400. Each group was assigned to one tool only — no individual LinkedIn profile appeared in more than one tool's outreach pool. This prevented cross-contamination between campaigns.

Each service was configured for its best available setup. For BriskReach, the Reps used AI-generated message drafts reviewed and sent by hand, referencing each recipient's recent LinkedIn activity, company news, or specific role context. LinkedSDR's verified operators worked from a comparable human-written approach. Akountify's marketplace agents wrote outreach to their own playbooks, while LinkedRent's shared-pool operators leaned on lightly-personalized templates using {first_name}, {company}, and {title} where supported.

Reply tracking used a dedicated inbox per tool. All incoming messages were reviewed manually; LinkedIn system notifications, bounce-type messages, and obvious auto-replies were excluded from the count. A reply was counted when the human recipient sent an intentional response within 14 days of the connection being accepted. Account health was logged weekly. Ban risk ratings reflect warnings, restrictions, or capability limits observed on each tool's LinkedIn account during the 6-week window.

Platform-by-Platform Engineering Notes

What each platform actually does, technically, and what that means for results.

BriskReach

Managed LinkedIn accounts, operated by real people

BriskReach takes a different approach from the other rental services in this test. Where Akountify is a marketplace of agents, LinkedSDR rents out verified profiles, and LinkedRent runs a shared pool of accounts, BriskReach provides dedicated managed LinkedIn accounts — called Reps — where one real person runs outreach on the client's behalf from an account built and maintained for their ICP.

Each Rep operates a dedicated LinkedIn account, built and maintained for the client's ICP. Connection requests are sent manually. Follow-up messages are written with AI-generated personalization, reviewed and sent by the Rep. From LinkedIn's perspective, the account behaves exactly as a human sales professional would, because it is one. That accounts for the 18.4% reply rate, the 62% acceptance rate, and zero account restrictions over six weeks — numbers that sit in the range of what a skilled human SDR achieves.

#2

Akountify

Fractional SDR marketplace — vetted human agents

12.7% Reply rate
52% Connections
Low Ban risk

Akountify is a fractional SDR marketplace: rather than handing over raw credentials, it matches your business with vetted outreach agents who run LinkedIn outreach on your behalf. Setup is quick and the entry price is the lowest of the services tested. During this test it produced a 12.7% reply rate and 52% accepted connections, with low account risk — no restrictions and a single soft warning across the window.

Because it is a marketplace, results vary with the agent you're matched to: the strongest agents rivaled dedicated managed accounts, while others were closer to template-grade outreach, which pulls the average down. Akountify is the right pick if budget is the gating factor and you can accept some variance in who actually runs your campaign. It's the best low-cost on-ramp to human-run, rented outreach.

#3

LinkedSDR

Verification-first rented profiles, vetted for quality

11.3% Reply rate
49% Connections
None Ban risk

LinkedSDR's differentiator is compliance. It mandates identity verification for every account on its roster and lists only highly-rated operators (4.5+ on G2), so every profile you rent is real, verified, and reputation-checked. That discipline is why it recorded zero account restrictions in the test — the safest competitor on the board — alongside an 11.3% reply rate and 49% accepted connections.

The trade-off is reach and flexibility: the verification gate keeps the operator pool smaller and outreach more standardized, so reply rates sit just behind Akountify's hand-matched agents and BriskReach's dedicated Reps. If account safety and provenance are your first concern — regulated industries, or simply risk-averse teams — LinkedSDR is the most defensible place to rent from.

#4

LinkedRent

High-volume rented profiles at the lowest per-account price

8.1% Reply rate
43% Connections
Medium Ban risk

LinkedRent is built for volume. Tiered plans from $140 to $190 per month give access to a shared pool of rented LinkedIn profiles, optimized for sending at scale rather than bespoke personalization. It produced the lowest reply rate of the four services (8.1%) and 43% accepted connections, with medium account risk — two activity warnings during the test, one requiring a brief cooldown.

The shared-pool model is what caps both reply rate and safety: profiles are reused across campaigns and outreach leans on templates, so messages read as less personal and accounts carry more accumulated activity. LinkedRent makes sense when raw volume and the lowest per-account cost outweigh reply quality — top-of-funnel list-building, for instance, where a lower reply rate is acceptable at scale.
